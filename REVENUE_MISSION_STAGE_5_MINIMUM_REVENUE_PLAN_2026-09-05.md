## Leanest first-revenue test

| Component | Plan |
|---|---|
| MVP scope | Sell and manually deliver one paid receivables diagnostic before building integrations. Review a customer-provided invoice-aging export; map the current follow-up process; identify leakage and exceptions; prepare an approved reminder ladder; define a promise-to-pay register; recommend the smallest implementation. Use spreadsheets and existing SHAKTHI document/report capabilities where safe. |
| Excluded from MVP | New SaaS UI, autonomous customer messaging, accounting-system integration, payment handling, legal escalation, bulk scraping, mass outreach, or unapproved production automation. |
| First-buyer profile | B2B service MSME with at least 10 recurring invoices monthly, visible overdue-payment friction, an accessible owner/finance decision-maker, and willingness to provide sanitized aging data under agreed confidentiality controls. |
| Channel 1 | Founder network and warm introductions to service-business owners/accountants. Prioritize trust because invoice data is sensitive. |
| Channel 2 | Publicly listed B2B service businesses found through Google Maps, LinkedIn and business directories. Research individually; record only observable evidence. |
| Channel 3 | Accountant, bookkeeper, CA, web-agency and IT-provider referral partners serving MSMEs. Offer the diagnostic directly or as a white-label delivery capability. |
| First lead batch | 10 named businesses: 4 warm/network, 3 public-directory prospects, 3 partner-referred prospects. Do not invent pain evidence and do not mass-spam. |
| Outreach structure | Specific observable signal → relationship-safe late-payment problem → offer a short receivables workflow review → simple request for a 15-minute conversation. Every message requires founder review before sending. |
| Timeline | Day 1: define evidence fields and build 10-prospect list. Day 2: qualify and prepare 10 individualized observations. Day 3: founder reviews messages. Days 3–5: send approved outreach and conduct conversations. Days 5–7: offer paid diagnostic to qualified buyers. Days 7–10: close and collect payment for one diagnostic, or apply kill/revise criteria. |
| Primary success metric | At least **one customer pays ₹14,900** for the diagnostic within the first 10 qualified contacts. Revenue counts only after payment is independently verified. |
| Secondary signals | At least 3 real buyer conversations; at least 2 buyers confirm the pain from their own workflow; at least 1 asks for a diagnostic/proposal. These are learning signals, not revenue validation. |
| Kill/revise criteria | Stop or materially revise the offer after 10 genuinely qualified, personalized contacts if there are fewer than 3 conversations; or after 5 qualified conversations if zero buyers confirm urgency; or after 3 diagnostic proposals if zero buyers pay. Do not respond by building more software. Record objections and test a changed segment, outcome, or price. |
| Data rule | Store only real prospect/customer facts with source and observation date. Mark unknown fields unknown. Keep invoice/customer data local and access-controlled; request the minimum necessary data. |
| Margin rule | Before accepting delivery, estimate founder hours, agent hours, API/software cost, hosting and support. Reject or reprice work that cannot plausibly maintain a healthy delivery margin. |
